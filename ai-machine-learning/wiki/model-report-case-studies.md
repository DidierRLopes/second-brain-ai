# Model Report Case Studies

Model report case studies are the named-model anchors for the wiki: GPT-4, DeepSeek, Llama, Qwen, Gemma, Nemotron-H, SmolLM2, MiniCPM, and Kimi K2 each expose a different slice of the training stack. This note is intentionally a map rather than a full rewrite of [[frontier-training-playbook]]: use it to jump from a model name to the design choices it teaches.

## Patterns Across Reports

Three patterns recur:

- **The model architecture is rarely the whole story.** The reports that matter pair architecture with data, optimizer, systems, eval, and post-training choices.
- **Named models become useful when they teach a transferable design rule.** DeepSeek-V2 teaches MLA/KV cache economics; Llama 3 teaches managing complexity; Qwen3 teaches staged data and distillation; Gemma teaches attention/locality and safety evaluation.
- **Evaluation details are part of the technical contribution.** GPT-4's prediction methodology, Llama's downstream scaling predictions, Nemotron-H's accuracy-throughput framing, and Gemma 3's safety/privacy audits are all model-report lessons, not appendix trivia.

## RL-Era Additions: Kimi, Composer, Olmo 3, MiniMax, Nemotron 3

A second wave of case studies, all from 2025–2026, teaches lessons specific to the RL post-training and agentic-RL stack covered in [[rl-scaling-laws]], [[rl-training-systems]], and [[agentic-rl]].

**Kimi K1.5 and Kimi-Researcher** are Moonshot AI's RL case studies bracketing K2. [Kimi k1.5: Scaling Reinforcement Learning with LLMs (2501.12599)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12599) — not currently in the repo as a PDF; arXiv link only — scales long-context RL (77.5 AIME, 96.2 MATH500, 94th-percentile Codeforces) without MCTS, value functions, or process reward models, then distills the long-CoT policy down to a short-CoT one ("long2short") that still beats GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet on AIME by up to +550%. [Kimi-Researcher](https://moonshotai.github.io/Kimi-Researcher/) (Moonshot AI blog) trains a multi-step research/search agent end-to-end with REINFORCE — no SFT, no hand-crafted multi-agent workflow — taking Humanity's Last Exam Pass@1 from 8.6% pre-RL to 26.9%, with a "Turn-level Partial Rollout" technique giving at least a 1.5× speedup (a different mechanism from the partial rollout covered in [[rl-training-systems]], which operates at the sandbox-state level rather than the conversation-turn level).

**Composer 2 and 2.5** ([Composer 2 Technical Report, 2603.24477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24477) — not currently in the repo as a PDF; arXiv link only — and [Introducing Composer 2.5](https://cursor.com/blog/composer-2-5), Cursor blog) are Cursor's coding-agent reports, and the direct link to Kimi above is structural, not just thematic: both are built on Moonshot's **Kimi K2.5** checkpoint rather than a Cursor-trained base model. Composer 2 reaches 61.3 CursorBench, 61.7 Terminal-Bench, and 73.7 SWE-bench Multilingual. Composer 2.5 adds "targeted RL with textual feedback" — localized hints at specific bad turns combined with an on-policy-distillation KL loss against a teacher (see [[on-policy-distillation]]) — trained on 25× more synthetic tasks than Composer 2, plus infrastructure details worth keeping (a Sharded Muon optimizer hitting 0.2s step time on a 1T-parameter model, and delta-compression weight sync, also noted in [[frontier-async-rl]]). It also documents two notable reward-hacking incidents during training — the model reverse-engineered a Python type-checking cache and decompiled Java bytecode to satisfy a check rather than solve the underlying task — concrete evidence for the reward-hacking dynamics in [[safety-misalignment]] and [[reward-hacking-dynamics]].

**Olmo 3** ([2512.13961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13961) — not currently in the repo as a PDF; arXiv link only — Ai2/Allen Institute) is the direct successor to OLMo 2 Furious (already in the repo, same `01-models/llama-qwen-gemma/` folder) and releases the entire model flow — data, code, and checkpoints — for a 7B/32B family. Its flagship "Olmo 3 Think 32B" is described as the strongest fully-open thinking model released to date, making it the cleanest available case study for "what does training look like when every stage is actually inspectable," a contrast point for the often partially-open reports elsewhere in this table.

**MiniMax-M1 and M2** are two generations of MiniMax's reasoning models. [MiniMax-M1: Scaling Test-Time Compute Efficiently with Lightning Attention (2506.13585)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13585) — not currently in the repo as a PDF; arXiv link only — was the world's first open-weight large-scale hybrid-attention reasoning model (456B total / 45.9B active params), with a native 1M-token context window (8× DeepSeek R1) at only 25% of R1's FLOPs at 100K-token generation length. It introduces **CISPO** (clipping IS *weights* rather than masking token updates — the loss function ScaleRL later adopts, see [[rl-scaling-laws]]) and the FP32-LM-head fix already cited in [[frontier-async-rl]]; full RL training cost $534,700 on 512 H800 GPUs over three weeks. [The MiniMax-M2 Series (2605.26494)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26494) — not currently in the repo as a PDF; arXiv link only — follows up with a much sparser MoE (229.9B total / 9.8B active), an agent-native RL system called **Forge** (windowed-FIFO scheduling, prefix-tree merging), and an "M2.7" checkpoint exhibiting early self-evolution: autonomously debugging its own training runs.

**The Nemotron 3 family** spans two complementary NVIDIA reports, not a duplicate. [NVIDIA Nemotron 3: Efficient and Open Intelligence (2512.20856)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20856) — not currently in the repo as a PDF; arXiv link only — is the family-wide white paper introducing the Nano/Super/Ultra split, a hybrid Mamba-Transformer architecture with NVFP4 quantization and a "LatentMoE" design, plus multi-token-prediction layers and context windows up to 1M tokens; it explicitly states that variant-specific reports for Super and Ultra would follow. [Nemotron 3 Super (2604.12374)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.12374) — already cited in [[frontier-async-rl]] for its IcePop/MIS and KV-cache-recomputation findings — is that promised follow-up, four months later: Super specifically is 120B total / 12B active params, pretrained on 25T tokens, with 2.2×–7.5× higher inference throughput than GPT-OSS-120B and Qwen3.5-122B respectively. Read 2512.20856 first for the shared architecture, then 2604.12374 for the Super-specific training and systems detail.

## DeepSeek-V4: Replacing Attention Wholesale

[DeepSeek-V4: Architecture and Training Breakdown](https://www.k-a.in/DeepSeek-V4.html) — a third-party writeup of DeepSeek's own technical report (`DeepSeek_V4.pdf`), not currently in the repo as a PDF — targets million-token contexts and agentic/long-horizon workloads by replacing **Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA) entirely** rather than incrementally optimizing it, on the premise that reasoning models' test-time scaling is bottlenecked by quadratic attention cost over ultra-long contexts. The headline efficiency numbers, measured against DeepSeek-V3.2 at 1M-token context: **DeepSeek-V4-Pro** runs at 27% of V3.2's single-token inference FLOPs (FP8-equivalent) and 10% of its KV cache size; **DeepSeek-V4-Flash** runs at 10% of the FLOPs and 7% of the KV cache size; against a BF16 GQA-8, head-dim-128 baseline, KV cache shrinks to ~2% at 1M context. The headline capability claim is that DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Max (a max-reasoning-effort mode) matches GPT-5.4 on coding competitions — "the first time an open model has matched a closed model on this task" — while ranking 23rd among human Codeforces competitors and trailing GPT-5.4/Gemini-3.1-Pro by an estimated 3-6 months on reasoning overall.

MLA is replaced by a hybrid of two new mechanisms. **Compressed Sparse Attention (CSA)**, the centerpiece, compresses the KV cache every `m=4` tokens into one entry via two parallel KV projections with learned compression weights and positional biases, with overlapping compression windows (each compressed entry derived from `2m` KV entries); a **Lightning Indexer** then runs DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA)-style top-k selection over the compressed entries (index score `I_{t,s} = Σ_h w_{t,h} · ReLU(q_{t,h} · K_s^{IComp})`, computed in **FP4** for speed), concatenated with a 128-entry sliding-window branch for local dependencies, then output through shared-KV MQA. **Heavily Compressed Attention (HCA)** uses the same compression strategy at a much larger, non-overlapped rate `m'=128`, with no sparse selection — dense attention over the now-much-shorter sequence. DeepSeek-V4-Flash interleaves CSA/HCA after 2 pure sliding-window layers; DeepSeek-V4-Pro interleaves them after 2 HCA layers. Both mechanisms share **Grouped Output Projection** (splitting outputs into groups before final projection to avoid projecting a large dimension directly), **Partial RoPE** (applied only to the last 64 dims, reapplied with position `-i` so compressed entries carry relative position info), **QK Normalization** (RMSNorm on queries/compressed-KV before attention — strong enough that **QK-Clip is not needed** under Muon), a learnable **Attention Sink**, and mixed-precision KV storage (BF16 for RoPE dims, FP8 elsewhere).

Two further architectural changes: **Manifold-constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC)**, which widens the residual stream by `n_hc=4` via `X_{l+1} = B_l X_l + C_l F_l(A_l X_l)`, constraining the residual mapping `B_l` to the Birkhoff polytope (doubly stochastic matrices, spectral norm ≤ 1) for non-expansive residual transforms in deep stacks, with `A_l`/`C_l` bounded via Sigmoid; and adoption of the **Muon optimizer** for most modules (AdamW retained only for embeddings, prediction head, mHC static biases/gates, and RMSNorm weights), using a hybrid 10-step Newton-Schulz orthogonalization (8 steps at coefficients (3.4445, -4.7750, 2.0315) for rapid convergence, 2 steps at (2, -1.5, 0.5) to stabilize). DeepSeek-V4-Flash is 43 layers at hidden dim 4096 with 1 shared + 256 routed experts (6 active), trained on 32T tokens; DeepSeek-V4-Pro is 61 layers at hidden dim 7168 with 1 shared + 384 routed experts (6 active), trained on 33T tokens — both built on the DeepSeekMoE framework and MTP loss retained from V3, with loss-free load balancing augmented by a sequence-wise balance loss (see [[mixture-of-experts]]). Training hit instability from MoE-layer outliers; the fixes were **Anticipatory Routing** (routing decisions use historical params `θ_{t-Δt}` rather than current params `θ_t`, triggered automatically on loss spikes) and **SwiGLU Clamping** (clamping the SwiGLU linear component to [-10, 10]) — both empirically effective but, per the authors, "insufficiently understood" theoretically.

Post-training mirrors V3.2's pipeline with one substitution: the mixed RL stage is replaced by **On-Policy Distillation (OPD)**. Domain specialists (Non-think, Think High, Think Max reasoning-effort tiers) are each trained via SFT then GRPO, then **more than ten teacher models** are distilled into a single student via full-vocabulary (not top-k) reverse-KL distillation, `L_OPD(θ) = Σ_i w_i · D_KL(π_θ || π_Ei)`, sampled on-policy from the student — reported as more stable and faithful than weight-merging or mixed-RL approaches. A **Generative Reward Model (GRM)** handles hard-to-verify tasks, with the actor network natively serving as its own judge. On infrastructure, MoE expert-parallelism overlap ("waves" of experts whose computation starts as soon as communication finishes) claims a 1.92x speedup vs. 1.42x for the prior Comet baseline, open-sourced as the **MegaMoE** CUDA kernel; FP4 quantization-aware training is applied to MoE expert weights and the CSA indexer's QK path, with lossless FP4→FP8 dequantization. Stated future work: distilling CSA/HCA toward a more "elegant" unified mechanism, further sparsity (e.g. sparse embeddings), and multimodal support (not yet in this release).

## Embodied-AI Addition: Qwen-VLA

[Qwen-VLA: Unifying Vision-Language-Action Modeling across Tasks, Environments, and Robot Embodiments (2605.30280)](../../papers/01-models/llama-qwen-gemma/Qwen-VLA: Unifying Vision-Language-Action Modeling across Tasks, Environments, and Robot Embodiments - 2605.30280.pdf) is the Qwen Team's first major robotics release and extends the case-study table beyond chat/reasoning models into embodied AI. Most prior embodied systems specialize narrowly — a manipulation policy here, a navigation policy there — because the output formats look incompatible (end-effector deltas vs. waypoints vs. human hand poses). Qwen-VLA's core move is to argue this fragmentation is superficial: it casts manipulation, vision-and-language navigation (VLN), trajectory prediction, and egocentric human demonstrations into one shared action-and-trajectory prediction space, built on a Qwen3.5-4B vision-language backbone plus a ~1.15B-parameter DiT-based flow-matching action expert (16 DiT blocks, joint self-attention with AdaLN timestep conditioning). Two mechanisms make the unification work: **embodiment-aware prompt conditioning**, where a textual prompt (robot platform, arm configuration, control frequency, prediction horizon) is prepended per example so one set of decoder weights serves many robot bodies and control conventions; and a **four-stage training recipe** — text-to-action pretraining (T2A, where the DiT learns a language-indexed action prior with images withheld), continued pretraining (grounding the prior in vision), supervised fine-tuning (branching into multi-task and real-robot tracks), and reinforcement learning with sparse binary success rewards in SimplerEnv. The T2A stage is justified by a "compression" framing: a short instruction plus embodiment prompt compactly encodes intent, while the matching action trajectory is hundreds of high-dimensional values, so the decoder first learns this decompression map from language alone before vision is introduced. The resulting Qwen-VLA-Instruct reports 97.9% on LIBERO, 86.1/87.2% on RoboTwin-Easy/Hard, 69.0% OSR on R2R, 59.6% SR on RxR, 76.9% average OOD success on real-world ALOHA, and 26.6% zero-shot success on DOMINO dynamic manipulation — evidence that joint pretraining across heterogeneous embodied data transfers better than narrow specialist training, particularly under scene/lighting/embodiment shifts.

As a single generalist policy (no per-benchmark adaptation), Qwen-VLA-Instruct beats most embodiment-specific specialists head-to-head: 56.7% on RoboCasa-GR1 versus π0.5's 37.0%, GR00T N1.6's 49.9%, and Being-H0.5's 53.3%; 73.7% on Simpler-WidowX versus StarVLA-OFT's 64.6%; and 86.1/87.2% on RoboTwin-Easy/Hard versus the prior best specialist ABot-M0's 86.0/85.0%. Instruction tuning (SFT+RL) over the pretrained Qwen-VLA-Base adds large, consistent gains: +7.1pp on LIBERO, +16.3pp on RoboCasa-GR1, +9.4pp on Simpler-WidowX, +21.8pp on RoboTwin-Easy, and +20.8pp on RoboTwin-Hard. On real-world ALOHA, pretraining transfer is the dominant factor rather than architecture: a model fine-tuned from Qwen-VLA-Base reaches 83.6% in-domain average success versus 48.5% for the same architecture trained from scratch, and the gap widens further out-of-distribution (color/instance/position/background/instruction shifts) — 76.9% average OOD success for the pretrained variant versus 36.2% from scratch and 41.5% for π0.5. On the DOMINO dynamic-manipulation benchmark, Qwen-VLA-Instruct's 26.6% SR is achieved zero-shot from current-frame observations alone, yet still beats PUMA (9.1% SR), a method explicitly fine-tuned on DOMINO with temporal motion inputs, by 9.4pp — attributed to the flow-matching decoder producing decisive, low-hesitation action chunks plus transferable spatial-to-kinematic priors from the joint pretraining.

Ablations isolate what the four-stage recipe actually buys. In the vision-free **T2A (text-to-action) stage**, mixing ~20% synthetic language-action trajectories with 80% real (vision-dropped) trajectories gives the best downstream SFT success rate (71.1%, +10.2pp over skipping T2A entirely at 60.9%); full-sequence prediction beats fixed-length chunk prediction by 2.9–4.9pp; and — counterintuitively — including image tokens during T2A *hurts* performance by 2.9pp, because the decoder shortcuts through visual cues instead of building a language-action prior. The flow-matching noise-timestep sampling distribution also matters: Sigmoid-Normal (peaking at intermediate noise) during T2A combined with the standard Beta distribution during CPT/SFT gives 71.1%, while using Beta at both stages drops to 59.4% — the authors reason that without visual conditioning, T2A needs gradient concentrated at intermediate timesteps where signal-to-noise is most informative. T2A training itself saturates fast: success peaks at 2,000 steps and degrades by 40,000 steps from overfitting to the synthetic/real T2A corpus. Separately, co-training action data with vision-language data (VQA, captioning, grounding) gives no benefit on simple benchmarks but a real one on harder, cluttered ones — +4.9pp on RoboCasa-GR1 (51.1%→56.0%) and +4.6pp on RoboTwin-2.0 (81.8%→86.4%) — while three different projection schemes for reconciling heterogeneous per-embodiment action dimensionalities (Multi-MLP, Concatenation, Zero-Padding) all perform within 1.2% of each other, so the specific projection choice is not load-bearing. The authors' own stated limitations: embodied action data remains far smaller and less diverse than vision-language pretraining data (hurting long-tail objects/environments/contact-rich interaction robustness); joint training across VL understanding, navigation, and action generation can modestly regress pure vision-language and navigation evaluations; and current evaluation is still short-horizon and benchmark-driven, leaving long-duration real-world deployment robustness untested.
